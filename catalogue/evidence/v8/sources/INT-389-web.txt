L198: FAQ
L199: What is Artificial Intelligence (AI)? AI refers to the simulation of human intelligence by machines. This can include problem-solving, learning, reasoning, understanding language, and even visual perception. AI systems don’t “think” like humans but are designed to mimic human capabilities through data processing. For instance, when you use Google Maps for directions, it uses AI to analyze traffic data and recommend the best route.
L200: How does AI differ from regular computer programs? Traditional programs follow a set of pre-written instructions to perform tasks, meaning they can’t adapt or change based on new data. AI, on the other hand, can learn from the information it's given and adapt its behavior. For example, a basic calculator performs the same function every time, while AI like Netflix’s recommendation engine changes based on what you’ve watched and what similar users enjoy.
L201: What are some everyday examples of AI? AI is deeply integrated into our daily lives, often in ways we don’t notice. Some examples include:
L202: 
L203:   * Search engines like Google, which use AI to provide relevant results.
L204: 
L205:   * Spam filters in email accounts, which learn over time to detect unwanted messages.
L206: 
L207:   * Voice assistants like Siri or Alexa, which understand and respond to spoken commands.
L208: 
L209:   * Streaming platforms like Netflix or Spotify, which suggest movies or music based on past behavior.
L210: What is Generative AI? Generative AI refers to algorithms that can create new content based on patterns they’ve learned from existing data. Unlike AI that only analyzes data, Generative AI can produce new text, images, music, and even code. For example, when you ask ChatGPT to write a poem, it’s generating new text based on its understanding of language from all the text it has been trained on.
L211: How is Generative AI different from other types of AI? Most AI is designed for analysis or decision-making, like predicting whether an email is spam or not. Generative AI is different because it creates something new. For example, if you give it a sentence, it can continue the sentence, or if you describe a scene, it can generate an image. It’s more creative in nature, which is why it’s used for tasks like writing, designing, or composing music.
L212: What does "generating" mean in the context of Generative AI? In Generative AI, "generating" means producing new content that wasn’t there before. The AI doesn’t just repeat or copy existing data—it uses what it’s learned to create original outputs. For example, if you ask a Generative AI to generate a painting of a sunset, it won’t pull an image from the internet but will create a new image based on its understanding of how sunsets typically look.
L213: How does Generative AI work in simple terms? Generative AI works by learning patterns from large amounts of data. It doesn’t understand things the way humans do, but it can mimic human creativity by recognizing how pieces of data (words, pixels, sounds) are typically structured. It’s like learning how to write by reading many books—it doesn’t understand the meaning of the words but knows how they fit together.
