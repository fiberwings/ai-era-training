L271: ## Description
L272: 
L273: AI is already making high-stakes business decisions—screening job candidates, setting credit limits, monitoring employees, writing customer messages, and powering chatbots 24/7. The upside is speed and scale. The downside is that when AI gets it wrong—biased outcomes, privacy violations, black-box decisions, or automated actions with no human recourse—it’s not the model that takes the hit. It’s your company.
L274: 
L275: And the risk is not hypothetical:
L276:   * 85% of consumers say they’re more likely to trust companies that use AI ethically
L277: 
L278:   * AI hiring and credit tools have triggered public scandals, lawsuits, and regulatory investigations
L279: 
L280:   * Privacy failures have wiped billions in market value and led to major settlements
L281: 
L282:   * New rules like the EU AI Act are turning “best practice” into legal obligations
L283: 
L284: So the real question is: how do you get the benefits of AI in business—without the blowback?
L285: In this course, Ethical Considerations in Business AI Applications, you’ll get a practical, business-first playbook for responsible AI. You don’t need to be a data scientist or a lawyer. You’ll learn how to spot ethical risk early, ask the right questions, and implement guardrails your teams can actually use.
L286: 
L287: You’ll learn how to:
L288: 
L289:   * Understand the core pillars of ethical AI: fairness, transparency, accountability, privacy, and human oversight
L290:   * Identify where ethical risks show up in common business AI use cases (recruiting, customer service bots, decision support, generative AI, and employee monitoring)
L291: 
L292:   * Detect and reduce bias using audits, representative data, feature review, and mitigation techniques
L293: 
L294:   * Protect privacy with data minimization, privacy-by-design, governance, and regulatory-ready practices (GDPR/CCPA)
L295: 
L296:   * Make AI decisions explainable with model documentation, reason codes, model cards, and tools like SHAP/LIME
L297:   * Build accountability and governance: assign system owners, create escalation paths, run reviews, and maintain audit trails
L298: 
L299:   * Apply real frameworks like the NIST AI Risk Management Framework and prepare for regulations such as the EU AI Act and U.S. sector rules
L300: 
L301:   * Turn principles into execution: policies, training, monitoring, and ongoing assurance so ethics stays “alive” after deployment
L302: Throughout the course, you’ll work through real-world cases (Amazon recruiting bias, Apple Card credit-limit controversy, Meta biometric privacy issues, Air Canada’s chatbot, Uber’s autonomous vehicle failure, and more) so you can recognize warning signs and respond with concrete steps.
L303: 
L304: By the end, you’ll be able to evaluate AI initiatives with confidence, reduce reputational and compliance risk, and help your organization build AI systems that customers and employees can actually trust.
