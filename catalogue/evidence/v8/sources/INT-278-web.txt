L140: ## Requirements
L141: 
L142:   * No prior AI or technical experience needed. This course focuses on judgment and management skills, not tools or coding.
L143: 
L144:   * You should currently manage people, even a small team, since the exercises ask you to apply the framework to real tasks your team does.
L145: 
L146:   * A notebook or document to complete the in-course activities.
L147:   * Familiarity with whether your company has an existing AI usage policy is helpful but not required. The course shows you how to find and use one if it exists, and how to proceed if it doesn't.
L148: ## Description
L149: 
L150: AI is already on your team. The question is whether you are managing it, or hoping it manages itself.
L151: Every day, managers across every function, HR, operations, finance, marketing, customer service, are quietly navigating AI adoption without a playbook. Someone on your team is already using ChatGPT, Copilot, or another AI tool for real work, and someone has already wondered whether that was okay to do. If you do not have a clear answer ready, silence fills the gap, and it never fills it the way you would want.
L152: This course gives people managers a practical, judgment-based framework for AI ethics, AI governance, and responsible AI use, without requiring any technical background. You will not learn to code or configure anything. Instead, you will build the manager-level skills that sit between company AI policy and your team's daily work: setting boundaries, making judgment calls, and leading the conversations that turn a written policy into something your team actually follows.
L153: 
L154: In this course, you will:
L155:   * Map the four AI risk zones, privacy, bias, accountability, and transparency, against your own team's real work
L156: 
L157:   * Build a three-tier decision framework (approved, needs review, off-limits) for sorting any AI-related task
L158: 
L159:   * Translate broad company AI policy into specific, actionable boundaries
L160: 
L161:   * Use a fast three-question checklist to resolve ambiguous situations without freezing or guessing
L162: 
L163:   * Lead a structured team conversation that sets clear AI usage expectations
L164:   * Build the psychological safety that gets people to disclose AI mistakes early, before they escalate
L165: 
L166:   * Contain, document, and escalate an AI-related incident in the critical first hour
L167: 
L168:   * Rebuild trust after an incident without overcorrecting into blanket restrictions and fear
L169: 
L170:   * Create a 30-day action plan to roll out and sustain AI guardrails on your team
L171: This is not a generic AI tools course, and it is not a legal or compliance deep dive. It is built specifically for people managers, HR leads, team leads, and L&D professionals who need practical AI management skills, real frameworks, real scripts, and real templates, not theory.
L172: 
L173: By the end, you will have a completed risk-zone map, a working boundary list, a team conversation script, and a 30-day rollout plan, ready to use with your own team starting this week.
L174: If you manage people in the age of AI, this course gives you the guardrails, the language, and the confidence to lead through it, responsibly and without fear.
L175: ## Who this course is for:
L176:   * People managers with direct reports who already have AI tools showing up on their team, with or without a formal policy in place.
L177:   * HR, operations, finance, marketing, or customer service leads navigating AI adoption, not just tech or engineering managers.
L178:   * Managers at mid-size to large organizations where a company AI policy exists but hasn't reached day-to-day team practice.
L179:   * Team leads who have faced a real moment of uncertainty, like a data-sharing question or a biased-feeling AI-assisted decision.
L180:   * New or first-time managers who want practical language and structure for AI conversations, not legal or technical theory.
L181:   * L&D and HR professionals building or delivering AI governance training for managers across their organization.
