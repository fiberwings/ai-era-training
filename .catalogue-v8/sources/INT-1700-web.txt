L311: ## Description
L312: 
L313: This course contains the use of artificial intelligence.
L314: 
L315: Generative AI is now inside customer chatbots, code assistants and document tools, and attackers have noticed. In this course you attack and then defend real AI systems, not just read about them.
L316: What to expect: about 5 hours of video. Allow 4 to 5 hours to run the 14 labs, or about 11 hours if you also complete every optional assignment. The five role plays are optional too; allow about 10 to 15 minutes for each one you choose to do. All 14 labs run in a single lab file on your own computer, using a free local Llama model through Ollama, so no API key or paid account is needed.
L317: Each lab has an optional assignment with a worked model answer; assignments are practice and are not required for your certificate.
L318: What you will do in the labs:
L319:   * Extract a secret from a chatbot's system prompt with prompt injection, and see why "never reveal" instructions fail.
L320:   * Hide instructions in a document and watch an AI summarizer obey them (indirect prompt injection).
L321:   * Compare jailbreak techniques, probe hallucinations, and test a model for bias with paired prompts.
L322:   * Build guardrails and run a 10-attack battery against them, then add output validation and a content classifier you can score for accuracy.
L323:   * Classify an AI system under the EU AI Act, generate an AI use policy, respond to an AI security incident, and run a full red team exercise.
L324: How the course is organized: nine sections take you from how generative AI works and the threat landscape, through prompt injection and jailbreaking, AI-specific attacks (data poisoning, model bias, SSRF through function calling, cost-based denial of service, hallucinations), and defense-in-depth, to secure development, red teaming, governance and compliance (EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF, ISO 42001), and emerging threats such as agentic AI.
L325: A running case study, NovaStar AI, ties every section to one realistic company.
L326: What you need: a computer that can run Ollama (Windows, macOS or Linux), a modern browser, and basic familiarity with security concepts. No programming or machine learning background is required.
L327: 
L328: Who this course is for: security professionals, penetration testers, developers building AI features, and risk and compliance staff who need to understand how AI systems are attacked and how to protect them.
L329: ## Who this course is for:
L330:   * Technology Professionals: IT professionals, cybersecurity analysts, and software developers looking to deepen their knowledge of AI-specific security challenges will benefit from this course. It provides the tools and insights needed to safeguard AI systems within their organizations.
L331:   * AI and Machine Learning Enthusiasts: Individuals with a keen interest in artificial intelligence and machine learning who wish to understand the potential risks and ethical considerations associated with these technologies will find this course enriching.
L332:   * Students and Academics: Undergraduate and graduate students studying computer science, artificial intelligence, or cybersecurity, as well as academics and researchers in these fields, will gain from the detailed exploration of AI vulnerabilities and mitigation strategies.
L333:   * Business Leaders and Managers: Executives and managers overseeing AI projects or considering AI implementations in their business operations will learn how to assess risks and implement effective security protocols to protect company data and AI investments.
